# 50M BF16 experiment

[run-50m-bf16.json](run-50m-bf16.json) is a fresh training run based on the published BF16 8 × 2 calibration. It is named `tinystories-50m-bf16` and uses Lab `903ad2f0bb261935132dee53295aba6b66f8e792`. It starts from scratch rather than resuming or warm-starting the FP32 baseline or calibration checkpoints, and those checkpoints are kept. When you record the run, include the Workshop commit that published the recipe and the native hash of the bound config.

The model has 50,556,800 parameters, seed 42, reference dense attention, BF16, microbatch 8, accumulation 2 and sequence length 256. The horizon stays at 244,141 updates / 1,000,001,536 targets. AdamW and its 2,048-update warmup are unchanged. Checkpoint every 256 updates with `keep_periodic=false`, and evaluate every 1,024 updates on at most 32 batches. Attention stays on the reference implementation; BF16 with SDPA is a separate test.

Reuse the pinned snapshot and the 4,096-entry tokenizer. At this Lab pin, prepare one native cache if none exists. Don't relabel old caches, download the corpus again or refit the tokenizer. Publish and verify an input bundle for this config, then stage through warmup with the public commands. Start from initialization and stop at step 256 with the full horizon in place. Verify that full-state checkpoint, resume it unchanged as a child run, compare the restored optimizer, RNG, cursor and counters, and continue to the original budget. Throwaway staging pilots don't count as training exposure. Even with periodic retention off, plan space for the current, best/initial and temporary checkpoints.

BF16 evaluation covers up to 65,536 targets (32 × 8 × 256), while the completed 5M evaluation covers 32,768. The two use different protocols, so don't rank their losses against each other. The calibration showed BF16 is faster. Whether the full run converges, how good its stories are, and whether it moves to remote machines or Colab are open until the run results are in.
